How to use the cross math maker

Pick a difficulty and a fresh puzzle appears instantly — every puzzle is different, and each one has exactly one solution.

  • Reading the grid: every row and every column is a complete equation — digit, operator, digit, equals sign, result. The digits are shown; some are filled in for you.
  • Solving: fill each blank with a digit from 1 to 9 so that every row and every column is a correct equation. There is always exactly one way to fill the grid.
  • Solve on screen: click any blank and type a digit, or use the arrow keys to move between blanks. Check highlights anything wrong.
  • Printing: “Print / PDF” prints the blank puzzle with its equations — up to 2 per sheet. “Print Answers” prints the completed grid.

The rules of cross math

  1. Fill every blank cell with a digit from 1 to 9.
  2. Every row is an equation: the first two digits combined with the operator must equal the result on the right.
  3. Every column is an equation the same way, read from top to bottom.
  4. Work strictly left to right (and top to bottom) — there is no multiplication-before-addition precedence.
  5. Division is always exact (no remainders), and every equation's result is a positive whole number.
  6. There is exactly one solution, reachable by logic and simple arithmetic.

Frequently asked questions

What is a cross math puzzle?

A cross math puzzle is a crossword-style grid of numbers where every row and every column is a math equation using +, −, × and ÷. Some digits are already filled in as clues; your job is to work out the missing digits so that every row and every column is true.

How do the equations in a cross math puzzle work?

Each line reads left to right (or top to bottom) with no operator precedence: digit, operator, digit, equals, result. For example, 2 + 3 × 4 means (2 + 3) × 4 = 20, not 14. Division is always exact — no remainders or fractions — and every result is a positive whole number.
