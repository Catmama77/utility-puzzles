Kakuro Maker

Kakuro is the number-logic cousin of sudoku — sometimes called cross-sums. Fill each run of white cells with digits from 1 to 9 so the run adds up to the clue in its black cell, with no digit repeated inside a run. Choose 8 × 8, 10 × 10 or 12 × 12 grids at three difficulty levels, solve on screen, and print or download as PDF with an answers option.

How to use the kakuro maker

Choose a grid size and difficulty, and a fresh puzzle appears instantly — every puzzle is different, and no two puzzles on a page share the same layout.

  • Reading the clues: in each black cell the top-right number is the sum of the white cells to its right; the bottom-left number is the sum of the white cells below it.
  • Solving: start with the shortest runs — a run of two cells with a clue of 3 must be 1 and 2, so any cell they share with a crossing run is instantly narrowed down.
  • Solve on screen: click a white cell and type a digit, or use the arrow keys to move between cells. Check highlights anything wrong.
  • Printing: “Print / PDF” prints the blank puzzle — up to 2 per sheet. “Print Answers” prints the completed grid.

The rules of kakuro

  1. Fill every white cell with a digit from 1 to 9.
  2. Each horizontal run must add up to the clue in the black cell at its left.
  3. Each vertical run must add up to the clue in the black cell above it.
  4. No digit may repeat within a single run — but digits may repeat across different runs.
  5. There is exactly one solution, reachable by logic and simple arithmetic.

Frequently asked questions

How do you play kakuro?

Fill every white cell with a digit from 1 to 9. Each run of white cells — horizontal or vertical — must add up to the number in the black cell at the start of the run, and no digit can repeat within a run.

Is the kakuro maker free?

Yes. Every generator on this site is completely free and unlimited. There is no account, no sign-up and no watermark, and you can generate as many puzzles as you like.

What do the clue numbers mean?

In each black cell, the small number in the top-right corner is the sum for the run of white cells to its right; the number in the bottom-left corner is the sum for the run of white cells below it.

How do I print a kakuro puzzle?
